1. Executive Scope & Objectives
DOC-09 defines the financial ledger and open-item data architecture used after a transaction is posted. It must preserve the difference between the accounting event itself and the settlement of that event. This distinction is essential for your ERP because a payment, credit note or debit note can exist before or after it is allocated to an invoice.
SOURCE DOCUMENT
   ↓
DOC-08 POSTING ENGINE
   ↓
JOURNAL + SUBLEDGER OPEN ITEM
   ↓
SETTLEMENT / CLEARING ALLOCATION
   ↓
OPEN BALANCE CHANGES
   ↓
OUTSTANDING / AGING / LEDGER / MIS

2. Design Principles
1. One posted financial event creates one authoritative accounting record; derived views may be rebuilt.
2. Open items are first-class records for parties that require bill-by-bill settlement.
3. Settlement allocation does not create a second payment or invoice; it links already-posted items.
4. An open item can be partially settled and remain open with a residual balance.
5. An on-account payment remains open until allocated; the allocation changes settlement state, not the original receipt amount.
6. Credit and debit notes retain their accounting direction while remaining eligible for controlled settlement where rules permit.
7. Outstanding is a calculated/read-model result, not a manually edited field.
8. Every financial adjustment must be attributable to a document, user, date, reason and source item.
9. Accounting, settlement and reporting must remain company-scoped and tenant-isolated.
10. All posting and clearing operations must be idempotent and concurrency-safe.
11. Historical postings are immutable in principle; corrections use reversal, adjustment, return or controlled reset-clearing workflows.
12. User experience may be simple, but the underlying data model must retain enough detail for audit and reconciliation.

12. Credit Notes & Debit Notes

12.1 User experience requirement
When the user is creating or viewing a credit/debit note, the document should offer an “Adjust / Allocate” action that opens the universal settlement workspace. This is the exact behavior requested in DOC-07.
12.2 Invoice-side floating adjustment
When viewing an invoice, the user should see eligible floating/unapplied payments and credit/debit notes and be able to allocate permitted amounts directly without leaving the invoice context. The system must record the same centralized allocation record used everywhere else.

15. Unapply / Reapply & Reset Clearing
Mistaken settlement must be recoverable. The preferred method is to reverse the settlement allocation relationship, not to rewrite the historical invoice or payment.
ACTIVE ALLOCATION
      ↓
UNAPPLY REQUEST
      ↓
VALIDATE NO FORBIDDEN DEPENDENCIES
      ↓
REVERSE ALLOCATION
      ↓
SOURCE OPEN AMOUNT RESTORED
TARGET OPEN AMOUNT RESTORED
      ↓
OPTIONAL REAPPLY TO CORRECT ITEM

29. Local-First & Live Cloud Compatibility
The ledger/open-item model must work identically on the local primary database and the live cloud transactional tier. Remote settlement actions must become visible to authorized local users and local postings must become visible to remote users after successful synchronization.

Final Design Rules for DOC-09
1. The General Ledger is authoritative for accounting balances.
2. The party subledger is authoritative for party-level transaction detail.
3. Open items are authoritative for settlement eligibility and outstanding detail.
4. Allocations are authoritative for bill-by-bill settlement relationships.
5. Outstanding reports are derived, not manually maintained.
6. Payments and credit/debit notes can remain floating until allocated.
7. Invoices can expose eligible floating payments and notes through the same settlement engine.
8. Unapply/reapply must preserve history and auditability.
9. No settlement operation may over-allocate an item.
10. All local/cloud financial operations must be idempotent and company-scoped.
11. Every future Billing, Purchase, Receipt, Payment, Credit Note, Debit Note and Report document must use this architecture rather than inventing its own settlement logic.
