DOC-17
PURCHASE, INWARD, VENDOR SUPPLY & ISSUE/COMPLAINT ENGINE
Developer-ready functional and architectural specification for purchase demand, authorized/fixed vendor rules, purchase orders, inward receipts, batch/expiry intake, vendor disputes, quality issues, claims, alerts, holds and integration with inventory, pricing, settlement and accounting.
Version: 2.0 - Revised purchase/inward specification with Vendor Supply Rules and Vendor Issue/Complaint Engine.


1. Executive Summary
DOC-17 is the procurement execution layer of the ERP. It converts approved demand/replenishment signals into controlled purchasing and turns supplier deliveries into traceable stock, claims and financial follow-up. The engine must support a small business buying directly from a vendor and a large C&F operation with multiple principals, branches, warehouses, fixed vendors, restricted supply, approvals and detailed quality controls.
The purchase lifecycle is document-driven. A purchase recommendation, request or order does not create stock. Stock is created only through an authorized inward/receipt movement after the receiving rules are satisfied. Vendor bills create or confirm financial obligations according to the accounting design in DOC-08/09; settlement of those obligations remains in DOC-07.

2. Non-Negotiable Procurement Principles
Purchase transactions must preserve company, branch, warehouse, principal/owner, product, batch, UOM and supply classification context.
No vendor can be selected merely because it exists in the vendor master when a product/principal has an active fixed or restricted supply rule.
Fixed/authorized vendor rules must be configurable by product, principal, territory, customer/supply channel and effective date.
Special/restricted/undercutting supply is a configurable business classification, not an automatic legal conclusion. The ERP must retain the classification and its reason while allowing the company to apply its own compliance policy.
Special/non-reorder quantities received under an authorized supply rule must flow into DOC-16 with the correct stock/supply classification so they do not inflate normal reorder calculations.
Every inward quantity must resolve to an acceptance state: sellable, quarantine, rejected, damaged, expiry-risk or another approved status.
Batch/expiry information must be captured at receipt where required; missing batch/expiry data should be a configurable hard stop or controlled exception.
Vendor complaints and disputes must be first-class records linked to documents, products, batches and amounts; they must not live only as free-text notes.
Vendor alerts and holds must be policy-driven, severity-aware and auditable.
Purchase-side CN/DN/payment allocation must use the existing universal settlement engine (DOC-07), not create a second allocation system.
Changes to an approved PO, received quantity, accepted/rejected quantity or vendor classification must preserve audit history and approval provenance.

5. Special / Restricted / Undercutting Supply Classification
The ERP must support the user-defined business case where certain fixed vendors provide goods to a restricted or unregistered supply channel at nominal pricing or transport-only economics, and those quantities should not be counted as normal reorder stock. Because terminology varies by market and policy, the system should treat "Undercutting / Special Supply" as a configurable internal classification, not a universal legal definition.

At PO and receipt time, the user should see a clear notation such as "Special Supply - Non-Reorder" or the organization-defined label. The classification must be carried to the receipt and stock movement so later users can understand why the quantity is excluded from normal replenishment.
6. Purchase Demand & Procurement Planning
DOC-17 consumes procurement demand from multiple sources and consolidates it without double counting. The same requirement must not be purchased twice simply because it appears in multiple demand feeds.

6.1 Procurement consolidation controls
Demand lines need a unique source reference and a status: proposed, approved, converted, partially converted, fulfilled, cancelled, expired.
Open PO quantities must reduce the outstanding requirement before a new PO is proposed unless the user explicitly authorizes duplicate/backup procurement.
Special/non-reorder demand remains separately identified from routine replenishment demand.
Unit-of-measure conversion, pack-size rounding, MOQ and vendor lead time are applied before the final order quantity is suggested.
Procurement suggestions should explain their math and source documents.
7. Purchase Requisition / Buy Plan
The requisition/buy-plan layer provides internal control between a demand signal and vendor commitment. Small businesses may bypass it through a configurable direct-purchase mode; large businesses can require it for specific products, amounts, principals or users.

10. Inward / Goods Receipt (GRN) Engine
The inward/GRN is the authoritative physical receiving event. It must support receipts against PO, ASN, direct purchase and approved returns where applicable. A receipt can be partially accepted, quarantined or rejected line-by-line.

13. Vendor Return & Purchase-Side Claims
Vendor returns are controlled reversals or outward movements from received stock. They must preserve the link to the original receipt/batch wherever possible. If stock was supplied by multiple vendors, the system must use actual provenance rather than assuming the newest or nearest source.

14. Vendor Issue & Complaint Engine
Vendor issues are formal records with statuses, ownership, deadlines and evidence. A complaint may originate from purchase, inward, stock quality, return, pricing, CN/DN, payment or another vendor interaction.

14.1 Complaint lifecycle
NEW → OPEN → INVESTIGATION → PENDING VENDOR / PENDING INTERNAL →
REMEDY IN PROGRESS → PARTIALLY RESOLVED → RESOLVED → CLOSED
Rejected and Cancelled are terminal branches requiring a reason. Reopened issues retain prior closure history instead of being overwritten.

16. Vendor Scorecard & Factual History
The ERP should provide factual vendor-history dashboards without forcing a single overall vendor score. The purpose is to let authorized users inspect documented performance and open issues.

17. Purchase Bill Readiness & Three-Way Matching
The ERP should support three-way matching for larger operations while allowing a simple direct-bill workflow for smaller businesses. The match is between PO, receipt/GRN and vendor bill.

18. Vendor Complaint Alerts During Purchase / Billing
When a user selects a vendor for a new PO or purchase bill, the system should surface relevant open issues before submission. The alert should identify the recorded fact, not silently make a procurement decision.

19. Purchase Return, Replacement & Replenishment Loop
Returns and replacement shipments must connect back to the original issue. A replacement receipt must not create a new unexplained demand or duplicate the original purchase requirement.

26. Security, Audit & Data Isolation
All records are tenant-scoped by organization/company ID.
Principal-owned stock and vendor data must follow company-level permissions and principal scope rules.
Vendor complaint evidence may contain sensitive commercial information and must honor role-based access.
Only authorized roles may override a fixed vendor rule, vendor hold or receipt exception.
Every override must record who, when, reason, previous state and approval reference.
Deletion of posted purchase/receipt history is prohibited; use cancellation, reversal or correction events.
Remote/cloud synchronization must use the platform-wide transactional/event architecture from DOC-04; documents need stable IDs and idempotent writes.

28. Acceptance Criteria / Definition of Done
User can configure a fixed vendor for a product/principal and the ERP enforces the rule during procurement.
User can configure authorized vendor lists, blocked vendors and time-bounded exceptions.
User can create a normal PO and a special/restricted/undercutting PO with explicit classification and reason.
Special/non-reorder supply received through the PO appears correctly in DOC-16 and does not increase routine reorder eligibility unless configured.
GRN supports partial receipt, batch/expiry capture, acceptance, quarantine and rejection.
Quantity, price, expiry, breakage and documentation variances can be recorded and routed to approval/issue workflows.
User can create a structured vendor complaint linked to vendor, product, batch and purchase documents.
Complaint statuses, severity, owner, due date and resolution are auditable.
Open vendor issues can trigger warning/approval/block behavior when a new PO or purchase bill is created.
Vendor returns and claims can be linked to the original received stock and resolved through replacement/CN/DN/payment.
Purchase bills can use PO/GRN matching and produce exceptions when tolerances are exceeded.
CN/DN/payment allocation continues to use DOC-07; no duplicate vendor settlement engine exists in DOC-17.
All purchase and issue records are tenant-isolated, role-controlled, keyboard-operable and crash/recovery friendly.
Every material decision is explainable from source documents, rules, approvals and audit events.
