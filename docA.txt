DOC-10
CUSTOMER & SUPPLIER MASTER FRAMEWORK
Business Partner, Accounts, Addresses, Credit, Commercial Defaults & Party Controls



1. Executive Summary
Customer and supplier masters are foundational records. Every invoice, receipt, payment, outstanding report, credit check, purchase bill, tax document and many operational workflows depend on them. The ERP should therefore avoid treating a customer as merely a name and phone number.

2. Relationship to the ERP Company Architecture
The master is company-scoped. Platform Admin can provision the capability and defaults, but company records belong to the Client Company. A Client Company cannot access or alter another company’s customer/supplier masters unless an explicitly authorized platform-level service account or platform function is used.

3. Business Partner Philosophy
3.1 Why a unified party identity
Established ERP designs increasingly separate the real-world party identity from role-specific customer/supplier data. SAP Business Partner is an example: the same business partner can hold customer and other roles, and current SAP documentation supports multiple addresses with address-dependent data. This reduces unnecessary duplicate records for one legal entity with multiple operating locations.
3.2 ERP rule for this project
ONE PARTY
   ├─ Customer Role (optional)
   ├─ Supplier Role (optional)
   ├─ Multiple Addresses
   ├─ Multiple Contacts
   ├─ Multiple Operational Sites
   └─ Multiple Financial/Commercial Profiles
Do not force users to create two different party identities when one legal/business entity is both customer and supplier.
Allow separate customer and supplier account numbers if accounting policy needs separate ledgers while preserving the common party ID.
Allow one party to have many addresses and designate their business purpose.
Keep transaction history attached to the role/account under which it occurred.

6. Multiple Addresses & Address Roles
A single party can have multiple business addresses. This is important for customers with head office, billing office, delivery sites, returns address and multiple branches. SAP explicitly supports multiple business-partner addresses and address-dependent data in current S/4HANA documentation.

6.1 Address behavior
One party can have unlimited practical addresses subject to configuration.
Each address has its own active/inactive state and effective dates.
The invoice can default billing and shipping independently.
Shipping address should be selectable at transaction time.
Address changes should not rewrite historical documents; documents retain their posted address snapshot.
If an address is used as a tax determinant, the selected address and its tax-relevant attributes are captured on the source transaction.
7. Customer Contacts

A contact can be linked to one or more addresses/sites and can have role-specific communication preferences.
8. Customer Credit Management
Credit is a central control because invoices can create receivables that later flow into DOC-07 and DOC-09. Mature ERP designs support both individual and group-based credit controls; Microsoft Dynamics documents customer credit groups that can assign shared credit limits while retaining individual customer limits.

12. Supplier Master — Complete Functional Structure
The supplier model should share the same party identity foundation but expose supplier-specific purchasing and payable settings.

13. Supplier Multiple Addresses & Contacts
Use the same address-role framework, with supplier-specific roles such as registered office, pickup point, billing address, factory/warehouse and returns address. This avoids creating duplicate supplier records just because a supplier has several locations.

38. Non-Negotiable Rules
1. One real-world business identity should not be duplicated merely because it has multiple addresses.
2. Customer and supplier roles may coexist under one party identity.
3. Customer and supplier balances are separate accounting roles unless an explicit authorized netting process exists.
4. Master changes must never rewrite posted historical transactions.
5. Credit and banking changes are controlled and auditable.
6. Party masters are company-scoped and protected by the ERP company boundary.
7. Every transaction uses the party/account IDs, not display names, as authoritative references.
8. Bill-by-bill settlement belongs to DOC-07; posting/open-item creation belongs to DOC-08/DOC-09; the master provides the accounts and defaults needed by those engines.
9. Batch/product/warehouse/transport data is not duplicated unnecessarily inside customer/supplier masters.
10. All master screens must support the keyboard-first UX rules defined in DOC-05.
39. Acceptance Criteria
Create a party once and add Customer role, Supplier role or both.
Create multiple addresses with distinct purposes.
Select a shipping address independently from billing address.
Prevent/flag duplicate GSTIN.
Set a customer credit limit and test warning/block behavior.
Create a shared customer credit group where configured.
Create customer and supplier accounts under the same party while keeping AR/AP independent.
Change a master address and verify posted invoices retain their original snapshot.
Block a customer and verify new sales behavior follows policy.
Import 1,000+ party records with duplicate preview and error report.
Search customer/supplier by keyboard using name/code/GSTIN/phone.
Synchronize a master update to the live cloud and verify remote users receive it.
Audit credit, tax, bank and status changes.
Merge two duplicate parties without losing transaction history.

Final Specification
DOC-10 establishes the party/customer/supplier master as the reusable identity layer of the ERP. Later documents should never create a competing customer/supplier identity structure. Sales, purchasing, billing, cargo, receipts, payments, accounting, settlement, reports and CRM should all reference this master framework.
