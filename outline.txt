Yes. For your ERP specifically, I would not start by building Billing or Accounting screens immediately. Your architecture is more ambitious because you have Platform Admin → independently isolated client companies + local-first/LAN + live synchronization + keyboard-first operation. Those foundations have to be designed before the business modules.
ERP modules are most reliable when their dependencies and data ownership are defined before implementation; finance, inventory and sales are especially interdependent, while permissions, audit and master data cut across all modules.

The actual build sequence
If you're asking me “Tell me exactly what I should work on one by one”, I would follow this:
One important correction to the numbering
There is a difference between designing something and fully implementing something.
For example, your Sync architecture must be designed in Phase 1, because every later module has to be compatible with it. But you don't need to finish every advanced synchronization feature before building Billing.
The same applies to:
Security
Database
API
Audit
Keyboard system
They are cross-cutting foundations and therefore get designed early, even though some of their deeper implementation continues throughout development.

What I would start with for you
I would not start with Billing yet, even though Billing is one of the most important modules.
Your immediate sequence should be:
STEP 1
MASTER PRINCIPLES
        ↓
STEP 2
PLATFORM ADMIN + CLIENT COMPANY
        ↓
STEP 3
LOGIN + COMPANY ACCESS
        ↓
STEP 4
USERS + ROLES + PERMISSIONS
        ↓
STEP 5
LOCAL SERVER + LAN
        ↓
STEP 6
SYNC ARCHITECTURE
        ↓
STEP 7
KEYBOARD-FIRST SYSTEM
        ↓
STEP 8
MASTER DATA
        ↓
STEP 9
ACCOUNTING CORE
        ↓
STEP 10
INVENTORY
        ↓
STEP 11
PURCHASE
        ↓
STEP 12
SALES ORDER
        ↓
STEP 13
BILLING
        ↓
STEP 14
CARGO
        ↓
STEP 15
FREIGHT / LOGISTICS
Then the rest follows naturally.
The reason is that Billing depends on Customer + Product + Price + Tax + Inventory + Accounting + Permissions, and Cargo depends on Billing/Dispatch + Transporter + Freight + Expenses. Building Billing first would create rework later. Integrated ERP workflows are most robust when data ownership and upstream dependencies are established before downstream transactions. 
So, for your project, I would consider these the five most critical foundations:
1. Platform/Company Architecture
2. Security & Permissions
3. Master Data Architecture
4. Accounting/Posting Engine
5. Inventory/Stock Engine
Then:
Sales → Billing → Cargo → Freight → C&F → Reports
That gives us a very logical build path instead of jumping from screen to screen.
