1. Executive Summary
DOC-35 defines the centralized freight and logistics cost engine for the ERP. It determines what a shipment costs, who bears that cost, how that cost is allocated, whether any part is recoverable, how actual transporter charges are reconciled, and how the result is exposed to accounting, reporting and dashboards.
A core distinction is mandatory: cost, recovery and profitability are separate values. A freight cost paid to a transporter is never silently replaced by the amount recovered from a customer, principal or another party.
1.1 Key outcomes
Support fixed, per-package, per-kg, per-quintal, per-ton, per-km, percentage, slab, zone and hybrid freight calculations.
Track loading, unloading, hamali, toll, parking, detention, insurance and other configured logistics costs.
Resolve the best applicable rate from transporter contracts and route/lane rules with effective dates.
Support estimated freight at order/dispatch stage and actual freight after transporter billing.
Allocate freight across consignments, invoices, challans, packages, weight, value or other configured dimensions.
Track customer recovery, principal recovery, internal subsidy and non-recoverable freight separately.
Allow the Platform Admin Company to bear client-company cargo cost and record it as an Admin Company expense while retaining client-level beneficiary reporting.
Provide auditable cost-to-recovery variance and logistics margin.
2. Design Intent & Problem Statement
C&F operations generate many freight-related costs that do not fit a single simple freight field on an invoice. The same dispatch may have base transport cost, loading, unloading, hamali, tolls and special charges, while the recovery may be fixed, negotiated, principal-funded, customer-funded or intentionally non-recoverable.
2.1 Problems solved
Avoid hard-coded freight formulas.
Avoid mixing transporter cost with customer recovery.
Avoid losing freight visibility when multiple invoices share one consignment.
Avoid double charging the same freight event.
Avoid silently changing historical freight when rate masters are later edited.
Provide a controlled way for the Admin Company to fund client cargo without creating unapproved client-company accounting entries.
Support small operations without forcing enterprise freight configuration.
3. Architecture Position & Ownership
4. Freight Cost Concepts
5. Cost-Bearer Model
DOC-35 introduces a first-class Cost Bearer Policy because freight may be borne by the Client Company, the Platform Admin Company, a Principal, the Customer, a shared service entity, or another configured party.
5.1 Non-negotiable rule
The identity of the cost bearer must be explicit for each freight cost event. A dashboard or report selection must not silently change the accounting cost bearer.
6. Freight Rate & Contract Master
A transporter rate card or logistics contract is a versioned, effective-dated master. Historical transactions retain the resolved rate snapshot and calculation inputs.
7. Lanes, Zones & Geography
Support origin/destination by address, city, district, state/province, country, postal code or configured logistics zone.
Allow explicit lane rules to override generic zone rules.
Allow distance source to be manually entered or supplied by an approved routing provider/integration later.
Store whether distance is estimated, contracted or actual.
Prevent ambiguous route matches by using priority/precedence rules.
8. Charge Basis Engine
9. Freight Calculation Engine
The engine resolves a deterministic calculation sequence. Every output stores the inputs and rule version used so the amount can be explained later.
9.1 Precedence
Explicit transaction override with approval
Customer/principal-specific contract
Branch-specific contract
Lane-specific contract
Zone-specific contract
Vehicle-type contract
Generic transporter rate
Company default freight rule
The selected rule must record the precedence path. If two rules have equal priority and conflicting rates, the system must not guess; it must flag a configuration conflict.
10. Ancillary Logistics Cost Engine
Every ancillary cost needs a cost type, source/reference and owner. Generic “other” charges cannot be posted without a reason code and audit record.
11. Cost Allocation Engine
One freight cost can relate to many commercial documents. DOC-35 must support allocation while preserving the original total cost.
11.1 Allocation invariants
Allocated total must equal source cost unless a configured residual/rounding policy exists.
Rounding residual must be assigned deterministically and auditable.
Reallocation after posting requires reversal/recalculation rather than silent overwrite.
A document cannot receive freight from a different company context without an authorized cross-company/cost-bearer rule.
12. Freight Recovery Engine
Recovery is independent of cost. The recovery engine determines what amount is billed or claimed from a customer, principal or another approved party.
13. Estimated vs Actual Freight
At order/dispatch stage the ERP may need an estimate. At transporter invoice/reconciliation stage the actual cost becomes authoritative for cost accounting. The system retains both.
14. Transporter Billing & Reconciliation
Import or enter transporter invoice/statement.
Match by consignment, LR/GR, vehicle, route, date and expected amount.
Support one transporter bill against many consignments.
Support one consignment split across transporter bills where partial/incremental charges are valid.
Flag unmatched, duplicate, rate mismatch, distance mismatch, weight mismatch and excess-charge conditions.
Create an approved variance rather than editing the transporter invoice to make it match.
15. Admin Company Funded Client Cargo
This is a key project-specific requirement. A Client Company may generate cargo and consignment activity while the Platform Admin Company is configured to bear the cost.
15.1 Required behavior
Admin Company records the accounting expense when the cost-bearer policy says Admin bears the cost.
Client Company retains the operational cargo/beneficiary attribution but does not automatically receive an expense entry unless a chargeback/recharge policy is enabled.
Every cost is tagged with client company, branch, consignment, transporter, route, cost type and period.
Admin can view costs client-wise, branch-wise, transporter-wise, route-wise, principal-wise, consignment-wise and period-wise.
Combined multi-client reporting is explicitly enabled by Platform Admin; it is never implicit.
Client-company users can see admin-borne freight only when their permissions allow it.
Optional recharge/chargeback can create an intercompany or service-recovery workflow without changing the original gross cost.
Changing the cost-bearer policy after posting must not silently rewrite historical accounting.

20. Tax & Localization
Freight may have tax consequences that vary by transaction type, jurisdiction and document structure. DOC-35 stores tax-relevant attributes and passes them to DOC-29; it does not become the tax authority.
Taxability flag and tax category for each charge type.

Tax-inclusive vs exclusive amount.
Tax component retained separately from base freight.
Effective-dated localization rules.

21. Inventory, Pricing & Billing Integration
DOC-34 supplies package, weight, route and consignment facts.
DOC-20 consumes recoverable freight and invoice presentation values.

DOC-15 may supply commercial pricing context where freight recovery uses customer-specific rules.
DOC-19 consumes freight status for dispatch readiness and transport assignment.
22. Data Model & Relational Schema
The following logical entities are required. Physical table naming may be adapted in DOC-45, but ownership and relationships must be preserved.
22.1 Core constraints
All cost events have an explicit company and cost-bearer context.
Historical snapshots are immutable after finalization.
Amounts use decimal-safe currency fields, never floating point semantics at the business-rule level.
Allocation totals are validated against source totals plus configured rounding residuals.
Contract/rule versions cannot overlap with ambiguous priority for the same exact scope.
A recovery cannot exceed recoverable base unless an approved override exists.

25. Local-First / LAN / Cloud Sync
Freight calculations should execute locally for active LAN operations whenever source data is local.
A calculation snapshot and event ID travel with synchronization.
Retries must be idempotent so freight is not duplicated after reconnect.
Conflicting rate-master edits require conflict handling; posted historical snapshots remain unchanged.
Remote users should see synchronized freight state and its data-as-of timestamp.
Admin combined reports must clearly distinguish synchronized vs pending data.
26. Error, Edge-Case & Recovery Handling
27. Controls & Audit
Every rate-master create/change/deactivate is audited.
Every manual override stores actor, reason and before/after values.
Every cost-bearer change stores effective date and approval.
Every allocation amendment preserves previous allocation history.
Every reconciliation keeps expected, actual and variance values.
Every admin-borne client cost is traceable to client, consignment and source document.
Every export of combined client freight data is audited.
No dashboard exclusion can remove a freight cost from accounting or audit truth.
28. Reports & Dashboard Feeds
DOC-31 may display these metrics only within the viewer's data scope and dashboard policy. The underlying freight facts remain in DOC-35 and financial truth remains in DOC-08/09.
29. Small, Medium & Large Business Modes
30. Performance & Scalability
Precompute reusable lane/rate matches rather than recalculating identical rules repeatedly.
Use effective-date and scope indexes for contract resolution.
Keep calculation snapshots compact but complete enough for explainability.
Aggregate reporting facts separately from transactional detail where justified.
Support batch calculation for end-of-day dispatches.
Prevent one large combined Admin report from scanning every transaction in real time when summarized facts are available.
31. Implementation Dependencies
32. Acceptance Criteria
User can calculate freight using flat, weight, package, distance, percentage, slab and hybrid rules.
Historical freight retains the exact rate/rule snapshot used at calculation time.
Actual transporter cost can be reconciled against estimated/accrued cost.
One freight cost can be allocated across multiple invoices/challans without double counting.
Customer/principal recovery remains separate from actual freight cost.
Admin Company can be configured as cost bearer for a Client Company.
Admin-borne cargo appears as Admin expense in the authorized Admin accounting/reporting context.
Client Company can see its beneficiary freight cost when authorized, without automatic client expense posting unless explicitly configured.
Admin can generate per-client, combined and transporter/route/consignment reports when authorized.
Combined reporting can be enabled/disabled and is permission controlled.
All manual overrides require reason and appropriate approval.
All important changes are auditable.
Offline and retry behavior cannot duplicate freight costs.
No future module creates a second freight calculation engine.
All dashboard/reporting calculations trace back to DOC-35 freight facts and the authoritative accounting records.
