DOC-16
INVENTORY, STOCK, EXPIRY, BREAKAGE & CLAIMS ENGINE
Developer-ready functional and architectural specification for sellable stock, non-sellable stock, traceability, returns, vendor/principal claims, reimbursement and configurable replenishment
1. Executive Summary
Inventory in this ERP is a movement-and-provenance system, not a manually editable balance. Every quantity must have a source, location, ownership, status and history. The inventory engine must also support non-sellable goods as first-class stock states so that expired and broken goods can be received, quarantined, claimed, returned upstream and settled without contaminating sellable inventory.

2. Inventory Philosophy & Non-Negotiable Rules
Every stock change must be represented by an authorized stock movement.
Stock balance is derived from movements and reservations, not manually overwritten.
Sellable, expired, breakage, quarantine, blocked, returned and scrap-pending stock are separate states.
Batch/lot traceability must be preserved wherever the product requires it.
Physical location and economic ownership are separate dimensions.
Customer-returned expiry/breakage can be accepted under a configurable policy even when the exact original sale cannot be proven; such quantities remain unverified/non-sellable until policy and inspection are satisfied.
Upstream vendor/principal claim attribution must not be guessed when multiple sources exist; the system must show provenance confidence and require adjudication where ambiguous.
Financial claim income/receivable should follow controlled recognition rules and must use DOC-07 settlement and DOC-08 posting rather than a second settlement system.
No expired or broken goods can be selected by a normal sales allocation process.
All claim, return, inspection and disposal actions are auditable.
Normal sellable stock and Reorder-Eligible Stock are separate logical pools. A quantity can be sellable for a specific transaction but excluded from normal replenishment.
Special/restricted/undercutting supply must be represented by a configurable stock or supply classification, not by hidden quantity exclusions.
Reorder calculations must use the configured Reorder-Eligible Stock pool and the selected demand formula; physical on-hand quantity alone must never drive purchase recommendations.
The reorder engine must support both simple configuration and user-defined formulas, with validation, versioning and an explanation of how each suggested quantity was calculated.
Vendor eligibility, fixed/authorized vendor rules and vendor complaint/hold logic are owned by DOC-17; DOC-16 consumes those rules when creating a reorder proposal but does not duplicate vendor-master logic.

9.2 Reorder & Replenishment Engine
DOC-16 is responsible for answering: "How much stock should be replenished, for which product/batch/owner/location, and why?" The calculation must be configurable by company, branch, warehouse, product, principal, supply classification and product category so that different businesses can use different reorder methods.
The engine must distinguish physical stock from Reorder-Eligible Stock. Normal reorder suggestions should normally ignore quantities classified as Restricted, Special Supply, Undercutting/Non-Reorder, Quarantine, Expired, Breakage, Returned, Recalled or other configured non-reorder states.
DEMAND / USAGE DATA
        +
LEAD TIME + SAFETY STOCK
        +
TARGET / REORDER RULE
        -
REORDER-ELIGIBLE AVAILABLE STOCK
        -
ELIGIBLE OPEN RECEIPTS
        =
SUGGESTED REORDER QTY
        ↓
VENDOR / SUPPLY-RULE CHECK (DOC-17)
        ↓
PURCHASE PROPOSAL / SALES ORDER COVER / EXCEPTION
9.3 Reorder calculation inputs & availability layers
Gross On-Hand: all physically held quantity, regardless of sellability or reorder classification.
Sellable On-Hand: stock that is currently allowed for normal sale subject to batch/shelf-life/ownership rules.
Reorder-Eligible On-Hand: sellable or otherwise configured stock that is explicitly included in replenishment calculations.
Reserved / Committed: quantity already committed to open orders or allocations and therefore not freely available for the reorder calculation.
Safety Stock: protected quantity defined by the selected reorder policy.
Open Receipts: confirmed purchase receipts, principal supplies or other incoming quantities that are eligible to cover future demand under policy.
Open Demand: approved sales orders, standing demand or other demand sources selected by the formula.
Lead Time: expected replenishment time from the applicable vendor/supply rule; when vendor is not yet determined, use the configured product/principal default.
UOM Conversion: all calculations must normalize to a calculation UOM and convert back to purchase UOM before document creation.
9.4 Formula builder and configurable reorder rules
A non-technical administrator should be able to choose a simple method or build a controlled custom formula from approved variables. The formula builder must show the resulting quantity before approval and provide a "Why this quantity?" explanation showing each input and intermediate result.
Example custom formula
Suggested Order Qty = MAX(0, (AVG_SALES_60D × Coverage_Factor) + Safety_Stock − Reorder_Eligible_Stock − Eligible_Open_PO)
The exact formula is configurable and must not be hard-coded as the only business rule. Circular dependencies, unsupported variables, invalid units and negative/overflow results must be blocked before activation.
9.5 Supply / Stock Classification
The system needs two related but distinct concepts: Stock Status describes the physical/quality lifecycle, while Supply Classification describes the commercial or operational treatment of the quantity. This allows a quantity to remain sellable while still being excluded from normal reorder calculations.
Important terminology rule: "Undercutting" is used here as an internal configurable ERP label for the special-supply case described by the business. It is not intended as a universal legal or industry definition. The system should let the administrator rename or configure the classification and its controls.
9.6 Special-supply / undercutting stock behavior
Specially supplied quantity can remain physically available and, where policy allows, sellable, but must be excluded from normal reorder calculations by default.
The transaction should store the classification at order, receipt, stock and invoice/challan level so that the quantity remains identifiable later.
A classification may override normal reorder inclusion, pricing behavior, claim eligibility or reporting without changing the physical stock location.
When the same product has both normal stock and special-supply stock, the inventory engine must maintain separate logical quantities so the normal reorder pool is not inflated by special-supply units.
Reorder proposals should display excluded special-supply quantities as an explanation line (for example: "12 units on hand, 8 reorder-eligible, 4 special/non-reorder").
9.7 Reorder proposal, exception and vendor-rule handoff
DOC-16 may generate a reorder proposal, but vendor eligibility remains governed by DOC-17. If a product has a fixed/authorized vendor, restricted-supply rule, territory/customer restriction or an active vendor hold/complaint, the proposal must display that condition before a purchase document is created.

   ↓

   ↓
CHECK FIXED / AUTHORIZED VENDOR RULES (DOC-17)
   ↓
CHECK OPEN VENDOR ISSUES / HOLDS (DOC-17)
   ↓

9.8 Reorder auditability and explanation
Store the rule version used for each calculation.
Store the exact input snapshot used for the calculation.
Store excluded quantities and the reason for exclusion.
Store the resulting suggested quantity, rounded purchase quantity and UOM conversion.
Allow the user to open "Why this reorder?" from the proposal.
Do not silently recalculate a submitted proposal using a newer formula version; retain the historical result and explicitly recalculate when requested.
Reorder rules must be company/branch/warehouse aware and respect tenant isolation from DOC-01.

20. Customer-Side Financial Treatment
A customer expiry/breakage receipt is not automatically the same as a sales return. The ERP should distinguish: (a) return of goods from an original sale, (b) market return accepted under policy, and (c) unverified/non-credit return.

31. Best-Practice Enhancements Added by Design
Provenance confidence score to avoid automatic wrong-vendor claims.
Separate sale lineage, purchase lineage and ownership lineage.
Non-sellable stock pools to prevent accidental sale.
Claim quantity reservation so the same quantity cannot be claimed twice.
Market-return path for commercially accepted goods not originally sold by the ERP company.
Inspection/disposition layer before financial credit is finalized.
Claim aging and pending-settlement reporting.
Batch-level traceability for recall and reverse tracing.
Integrated settlement through DOC-07 instead of duplicating financial allocation logic.
Company/principal/vendor policy rules so small businesses can keep the workflow simple while large businesses can enable full controls.

34. Final Acceptance Criteria
No expired or broken quantity can enter ordinary sellable availability without explicit controlled release.
Every non-sellable quantity is traceable to a source event.
Every customer expiry/breakage receipt can show batch history and prior sales/returns when available.
Every upstream vendor/principal claim can show source receipts, batch lineage and prior claim usage.
The same source quantity cannot be claimed twice.
Original-sale returns and market returns remain distinguishable.
Vendor/principal claim settlement can use CN, DN, payment or replacement stock.
Financial allocation uses DOC-07; posting uses DOC-08; open items use DOC-09.
Batch/provenance data remains synchronized for remote users under DOC-04.
All exceptions and overrides are auditable.
Reorder suggestions use Reorder-Eligible Stock, not raw physical stock.
Special/undercutting/non-reorder supply can be excluded from normal replenishment without removing the physical quantity from inventory.
Different products, warehouses or businesses can use different reorder methods and approved custom formulas.
Every reorder recommendation records its formula/rule version, inputs, excluded quantities and explanation.
Reorder proposals hand off fixed-vendor, restricted-supply and vendor-hold checks to DOC-17 rather than duplicating vendor logic in DOC-16.
35. Reference ERP Patterns Used for Design
This document uses established ERP/WMS patterns as reference points while tailoring them to the C&F use case. SAP documents batch traceability, customer-return inspection and return-to-vendor workflows; Microsoft Dynamics documents quarantine, disposition and return inspection; Odoo documents lot-based expiry lifecycle controls. These references are patterns, not a prescription to copy any product UI or implementation.
