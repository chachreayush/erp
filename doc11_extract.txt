PRODUCT, SKU & BATCH MASTER FRAMEWORK
Master-data architecture for products, variants, UOM, packaging, pricing hooks, traceability and batch-controlled inventory
1. Purpose & Scope
This document defines the functional and architectural requirements for Product, SKU, Variant, UOM, Packaging, Pricing hooks and Batch master data. It is intentionally detailed enough to guide screen design, database design, APIs, validation and later transaction-module documents.
1.1 What this master must achieve
One authoritative product identity per company context.
Clear separation between product-level facts and batch-level facts.
Fast search and selection for billing, purchase and warehouse operators.
Strong traceability from receipt to dispatch, invoice, return and recall.
Support for small-business simplicity and large-business depth without two different data models.
Safe integration with DOC-07 bill-by-bill settlement indirectly through transaction line identity; product master itself must never carry payment state.

3. Product Identity Levels
3.1 Product vs SKU decision
The ERP should support both simple and advanced modes. For a small business, Product and SKU may appear as one screen. For larger operations, Product Family → Product Master → Variant/SKU can be enabled. Internal IDs remain stable regardless of display mode.

5. Unit of Measure (UOM) Architecture
The product must have a base unit of measure and may have alternative units used for purchase, sale, dispatch and reporting. Established ERP systems support centrally maintained product units and alternative conversions; SAP also documents batch-specific unit conversions where a batch can have an actual ratio different from the planned product ratio. Odoo likewise supports multiple units and packagings.
5.1 Conversion architecture
PURCHASE UOM → BASE UOM ← SALES UOM
             ↑
     BATCH-SPECIFIC FACTOR
     (only where permitted)
Conversion must be mathematically consistent and decimal-safe.
Conversion changes must be audited.
Product-level planned conversion can exist as a default; batch-specific actual conversion can override when the business case requires it.
The transaction should show both entered UOM/quantity and normalized base quantity.
Do not silently change a historical conversion used by a posted transaction.

7. Product Attributes & Variants
Advanced businesses may need multiple variants of a product master. Microsoft Dynamics 365 documents product masters and variants generated from dimension values; the ERP can use the same conceptual pattern while keeping the first release simpler.
8. Product ↔ Batch Architecture
Batch must be treated as a child traceability record linked to a Product/SKU. Batch management is particularly important where expiry, recall, quality, ownership, or batch-specific price/MRP applies. SAP describes batches as uniform traceable quantities and highlights recall and expiration use cases; Odoo similarly uses lots/serials with expiration tracking.
PRODUCT: SKU-0001
     │
     ├── Batch A123 → MFG / EXP / MRP / stock / supplier
     ├── Batch A124 → MFG / EXP / MRP / stock / supplier
     └── Batch A125 → MFG / EXP / MRP / stock / supplier
9. Batch Numbering & Uniqueness
Batch number should not be globally unique across every product unless configured; recommended default is Product + Batch Number within Company/Principal scope.
The system should detect duplicate batch numbers when an inward entry is created.
If the same batch arrives multiple times, use the same batch record with multiple stock movements rather than creating duplicate batch masters.
For principal-owned stock, principal should be included in the uniqueness/ownership context when business rules require it.
Batch import should validate duplicate combinations before commit.

       ↓

       ↓
WAREHOUSE STOCK
       ↓
SALES / DISPATCH
       ↓

       ↓
CUSTOMER

Reverse trace:
Customer / Invoice → Batch → Receipt → Supplier / Principal
Every stock movement must carry product and batch identity when batch tracking is enabled.
Every posted invoice line using a batch should be traceable back to the batch stock movement.
Returns must preserve original batch where possible.
Recall reporting must identify affected stock, documents, customers and remaining quantity.
12. Batch Allocation Policies
FEFO should be a recommendation engine rather than an invisible forced choice. The user should see the proposed batches, available quantity and expiry, then confirm unless company policy requires automatic enforcement.

14. MRP & Smart Price Intelligence
The product master should provide a stable place for MRP-related rules, while actual transaction-time MRP may be determined at batch/variant level when applicable. The system should never blindly overwrite a batch MRP with a product-level value.
14.1 Smart MRP / pricing suggestions
Compare product master price, price list, customer history, principal rule and batch MRP.
Show the reason for the suggested rate.
Never allow a suggestion engine to bypass statutory constraints or configured approval ceilings.
Keep the original source price and final applied rate for audit.

21. Product Lifecycle & Versioning
DRAFT → ACTIVE → BLOCKED / INACTIVE → ARCHIVED
Draft products cannot be used in posting transactions unless company policy permits.
Inactive products should not appear in new transactional search but remain visible in historical documents.
Blocked products should explicitly show a block reason.
Historical invoices must retain the original product description/rate context used at posting.
Master changes that alter accounting/tax behavior should be audited.
22. Local-First & Live Cloud Synchronization
The product master must be safe to edit on the local primary system while its compact, necessary data is propagated to the live cloud transaction tier for remote users. Synchronization should preserve product IDs, version numbers and batch relationships.
LOCAL PRODUCT CHANGE
      ↓
CHANGE EVENT
      ↓
CLOUD TRANSACTIONAL STORE
      ↓
REMOTE USER SEES UPDATED PRODUCT/BATCH
      ↓
ACK / VERSION CONFIRMATION
      ↓
LOCAL STATUS = SYNCED
Do not allow two users to create two different internal IDs for the same master after synchronization.
Use immutable IDs and version metadata.
Conflict resolution must be explicit for concurrent edits.
Batch stock quantity itself is not edited from a product master screen; stock movement is authoritative.

32. Governance Rules
1. Do not duplicate a product solely because a principal/customer has a different code.
2. Do not store stock balances as editable master values.
3. Do not store payment/settlement information in Product or Batch.
4. Do not delete a product/batch that is referenced by posted transactions.
5. Do not change historical batch facts in a way that rewrites the meaning of a posted transaction.
6. Do not change base UOM casually after movements exist.
7. Do not allow price/MRP overrides without controlled permissions where configured.
8. Keep product, batch, stock movement and accounting records logically separate but linked.
