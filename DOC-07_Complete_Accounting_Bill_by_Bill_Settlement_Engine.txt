1. Purpose and Scope
DOC-07 defines the accounting foundation used by every later financial and operational module. It covers the Chart of Accounts, General Ledger, receivable/payable subledgers, document balances, bill-by-bill allocation, payment adjustment, credit/debit note adjustment, on-account money, netting, reconciliation and controls.

2. Accounting Philosophy
BUSINESS EVENT
   ↓
DOCUMENT / TRANSACTION
   ↓
CALCULATION
   ↓
POSTING
   ↓
OPEN PARTY ITEM
   ↓
SETTLEMENT / ADJUSTMENT
   ↓
REMAINING OUTSTANDING
   ↓
REPORTS / FINANCIAL STATEMENTS
Accounting must be generated from business events instead of manually maintaining parallel balances.
Every posted journal must balance.
Invoices, credit notes, debit notes and payments are permanent financial events; settlement only links or offsets open items.
Unallocated money must remain visible as an on-account credit/debit instead of disappearing into a total balance.
Outstanding reports should be calculated from open items after posted allocations, not from manually edited totals.
The same customer account must support many invoices, many receipts, credit notes, debit notes and mixed settlements.
The accounting engine must support both small-business simplicity and large-business controls.

6. System-Protected Ledgers
System-created core ledgers must not be deleted if referenced.
Users may deactivate rather than destroy a used ledger.
Core control accounts should have protected mappings.
Changes to sensitive mappings require elevated permission and audit.
A client company can configure its own operational accounts but cannot alter platform-level financial security rules.

Every source document has a traceable posting reference.
Reversal uses a new controlled entry rather than deleting history.
Posted journal lines are immutable to ordinary users.

9. Bill-by-Bill Architecture
Bill-by-bill means that an account can show every open invoice and every available credit/debit item separately, with a settlement amount against each. The balance is the sum of remaining open amounts, not simply one running number.

INV-004   ₹12,000   → OPEN ₹12,000

TOTAL OUTSTANDING = ₹28,000

12. Floating / On-Account Payments
A “floating payment” is an amount received from a party but not yet assigned to a specific invoice, or an amount deliberately left as a credit for future settlement.

12.1 Automatic suggestion
Suggest oldest due invoice first (configurable).
Suggest exact-reference invoice if payment reference matches.
Suggest same customer only.
Show confidence/reason for suggestion.
Never auto-post an uncertain allocation without the configured approval rule.
13. Credit Note Allocation
A credit note reduces the amount receivable from a customer. It should be possible to create it from an invoice, from the credit note screen, or from the party settlement workspace. Credit notes can be partially or fully allocated to one or several invoices.
CREDIT NOTE ₹5,000
     ↓
AVAILABLE CREDIT
     ├─ INV-101 → ₹3,000
     └─ INV-103 → ₹2,000
     ↓
CN CLOSED
INVOICE BALANCES REDUCED
13.1 Invoice ↔ Credit Note
If a user opens an invoice, the invoice screen should show an “Available Credits / Adjustments” panel with eligible credit notes. If the user opens the credit note, the same engine should show eligible invoices. Both routes produce the same allocation records.
14. Debit Note Allocation
A debit note normally increases the amount owed by the party. Therefore, in a customer receivable subledger, a customer debit note is a debit-side open item. It can be linked to/reference the invoice that caused the adjustment, but it should not be falsely treated as a payment or credit.

38. Failure & Edge Cases
Payment arrives without invoice reference → on-account credit.
Payment amount exceeds selected invoices → remaining amount stays on-account unless user chooses approved refund/write-off.
Credit note exceeds one invoice → allocate across multiple invoices.
Credit note exceeds all current invoices → remaining credit stays available.
Payment + CN both applied to the same invoice → supported through multiple allocation lines.
User attempts to apply more than remaining balance → block or auto-cap with explicit confirmation.
Two users try to allocate the same credit simultaneously → concurrency check prevents double allocation.
Network retry repeats a settlement request → idempotency prevents duplicate allocation.
Invoice is cancelled after being partially settled → settlement must be reversed/reallocated before final cancellation as policy requires.
A party has a net credit balance → outstanding report must distinguish credit available from amount due.

Appendix C. Recommended Default Rules
1. Every customer/supplier financial document creates an open-item record when posted, except documents that are inherently non-open-item.
2. Open items are immutable facts; settlement allocations are separate records.
3. Invoice screens show eligible credits/payments without requiring the user to leave the invoice.
4. Payment screens default to bill-by-bill allocation but permit on-account posting.
5. Credit notes can be allocated from the credit-note screen or invoice screen.
6. Debit notes are treated as debit-side items and may be linked/referenced, never silently treated as credits.
7. All settlement amounts must be capped by both source and target remaining balances.
8. Unapply/reversal must restore exact open amounts.
9. Outstanding report defaults to open debits but can show available credits separately.
10. Auto-match should be suggestions first unless a company explicitly enables trusted auto-post rules.
11. Write-off requires reason and permission.
12. Every settlement allocation receives a unique ID and audit trail.
13. Cloud/local retries must be idempotent.

Completeness Addendum — Universal Settlement & Adjustment Model
The core requirement of this ERP is that bill-by-bill settlement must be a universal capability, not a separate feature duplicated inside Payment, Invoice, Credit Note, Debit Note and Outstanding screens. One settlement engine must maintain the open-item state for every party and every eligible financial document.
INVOICE / BILL ↕ PAYMENT / RECEIPT ↕ CREDIT NOTE ↕ DEBIT NOTE ↕ ADVANCE / ON-ACCOUNT ↕ WRITE-OFF / ADJUSTMENT
Matching existing posted documents changes allocation/open status. It must not create artificial sales, purchases, cash, tax or stock movements. A genuine accounting change remains a separate controlled document.

OPEN RECEIPT → Adjust → open invoices + eligible notes → select target bills → preview → save

D. Invoice-Level Floating Adjustment Panel
Every posted invoice should have a settlement panel so a user can see every available credit/payment and every remaining open amount without leaving the invoice.

E. Floating / On-Account Payments
Allow payment entry even when the exact invoice is not yet known.
Store the unallocated amount against the correct party as On-Account / Advance.
Show the available on-account balance on the party dashboard and settlement workspace.
Allow later allocation to one or many invoices.
Allow partial allocation while retaining the remaining amount as floating.
Prevent allocation above available source amount or target outstanding.
Show clear source and target balances before saving.
F. Credit Note ↔ Invoice Adjustment
A credit note can be created against an invoice and can also remain unallocated for future eligible invoices when business rules allow. Allocation should reduce the selected invoice outstanding without rewriting the original invoice.
Credit Note ₹5,000 → Apply to INV-002 ₹15,000 → INV-002 remaining ₹10,000
A single credit note may be split across multiple invoices where permitted. A single invoice may receive multiple credit notes. Every allocation is separately stored.
G. Debit Note Rules
Debit notes must keep their accounting direction. For a customer, a debit note generally increases the amount receivable; for a supplier, a debit note generally increases the amount recoverable from the supplier or reduces the payable depending on the transaction type. The settlement engine may use eligible debit notes for netting/offsetting, but must not treat them as cash.

L. Unapply / Reapply
Authorized users should be able to unapply a settlement allocation without cancelling the original posted payment, invoice or note. The system restores the appropriate available/open amounts, records the unapply reason and audit event, and permits corrected reallocation.

M. Outstanding Engine
The open-outstanding view should be generated from posted open items and settlement allocations. It should not rely on a separate manually maintained balance field.
OPEN ITEM = POSTED DOCUMENT AMOUNT − VALIDATED ALLOCATIONS − OTHER AUTHORIZED CLEARING

N. Dispute / Hold
Allow dispute flag and reason without changing the accounting amount.
Optionally prevent auto-match for disputed items.
Show dispute owner and follow-up date.
Keep dispute status separate from accounting settlement status.
O. Write-Offs and Small Differences
Small residuals created by approved rounding, charges or commercial differences should use a dedicated write-off/adjustment workflow with permissions, approval thresholds and an account mapping. The original invoice/payment must remain traceable.

Q. Accounting Integrity
Allocation links existing posted documents. It must not create artificial revenue, expense, cash, inventory or tax. If the business event itself requires a new accounting adjustment, the adjustment is posted separately and then may become eligible for settlement.
R. Reconciliation Controls
Allocated amount cannot exceed source available amount.
Allocated amount cannot exceed target open amount.
Company and party must match unless explicitly allowed.
Currency must match unless an approved FX process is used.
Closed/reversed documents are not eligible for new allocation.
Every allocation has source document ID + target document ID + amount + timestamp + user.
Concurrent allocation attempts must be protected against over-allocation.

T. Reference Patterns
Established ERP/accounting systems commonly expose an allocation/reconciliation workflow where open invoices and unapplied payments or credits are presented together, exact amounts are allocated, and the allocation reduces the open balance without generating a second payment or sale. ERPNext documents reconciliation between invoices, payments and credit notes; Oracle documents allocation of payments against open items; SAP documents clearing/payment allocation patterns. These are reference patterns, not copied implementations.
U. Final Acceptance Criteria — Settlement
1. User can select exactly which invoice a payment settles.
2. Fully settled invoices disappear from normal open outstanding but remain in history.
3. Partial payment reduces only the selected invoice by the allocated amount.
4. One payment can settle multiple invoices.
5. Multiple payments can settle one invoice.
6. Credit notes can be allocated to invoices and remain traceable.
7. Debit notes retain the correct accounting direction.
8. Payment, invoice, credit note and debit note can open the same settlement workspace.
9. Floating/on-account balances remain available until allocation.
10. Unapply/reapply restores balances correctly and records an audit event.
11. Outstanding is calculated from authoritative posted open items and allocation state.
12. No settlement can cross company boundaries or invalid parties/currencies.
13. Allocation is safe under concurrent users and retry/replay.
14. Every allocation is traceable from source to target and back.
